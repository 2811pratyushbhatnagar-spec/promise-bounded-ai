# Before You Ask AI

A one-minute reset for making clearer AI requests.

AI can make things easier.

But easier for what?

Before asking AI to do something, pause and ask:

## 1. What is the promise?

What am I asking the AI to help carry, change, decide, create, summarize, judge, repair, or send?

What should this request actually do?

## 2. What boundary should be respected?

Whose privacy, consent, attention, dignity, time, authorship, safety, livelihood, or emotional reality is involved?

What should the AI not cross?

## 3. What cost might be hidden?

Will this request shift cost onto someone else?

Cost can be time, labor, emotion, confusion, dependency, reputation, ecological load, or future repair.

## 4. What trace should remain?

What should be visible afterward?

A summary? A source? A decision note? A draft that can be reviewed? A record of uncertainty? A clear line saying what was not checked?

## 5. What repair path should be possible?

If the AI gets it wrong, who can notice?

Who can correct it?

What should be reversible?

What needs human review before it affects another person?

---

Then ask.

The goal is not a perfect prompt.

The goal is a request that does not hide its promise.

## Short form

Before making an AI request, ask:

What is the promise?
What boundary should be respected?
What cost might be hidden?
What trace should remain?
What repair path should be possible?

AI lowers activation energy.

This asks: for what?

## Example

Instead of:

> Write a message to my coworker saying they are wrong.

Try:

> Help me draft a message to my coworker. The promise is to name a disagreement clearly without humiliating them. Respect their dignity and my boundary. Hidden costs might include blame, defensiveness, or making the conflict harder to repair. Leave me with a draft I can review before sending. Include a repair path if the message sounds too harsh.

## Boundary

This is not a theory of AI.

It is not a complete safety system.

It is not a replacement for judgment.

It is one small tool for making one request clearer.

Use it where it reduces distortion.

Set it down where it becomes another thing to defend.
